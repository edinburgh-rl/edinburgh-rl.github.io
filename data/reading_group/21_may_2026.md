title:  Inter-Agent Relative Representations for Multi-Agent Option Discovery
abstract: Temporally extended actions are essential for efficient exploration, yet the exponential complexity of multi-agent environments often forces a trade-off between coordination and scalability. We are excited to present a novel framework for multi-agent option discovery that overcomes these limitations! Our approach introduces a sophisticated joint-state abstraction that compresses the environment while retaining the vital synchronization patterns necessary for high-level teamwork. By approximating a 'Fermat' state  of maximal team alignment, we define a unique measure of 'spreadness' to capture misalignment across the group. We then leverage a neural graph Laplacian estimator to derive options that foster organic state synchronization. Across multiple simulated domains, we demonstrate that these learned options provide a significant leap in downstream coordination capabilities compared to traditional methods.

bio: Raul is a third-year PhD student at the Edinburgh Centre for Robotics, where he is part of the Robotics and Autonomous Systems CDT and is advised by Mohan Sridharan. He is also a member of MARBLE, an interest group focused on Reinforcement Learning. His research interests lie primarily in reinforcement learning, with a particular emphasis on temporally extended actions, including options, skills, and macro-actions, as well as hierarchical reinforcement learning and multi-agent systems. His recent work brings these themes together by studying the discovery and reuse of task-agnostic coordinated behaviours in multi-agent reinforcement learning. He believes that coordinated behaviours give agent teams the strongest head start when it comes to finding better solutions in downstream tasks.
